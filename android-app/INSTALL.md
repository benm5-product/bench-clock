# Installing Bench Clock on your Android phone

Bench Clock isn't in the Play Store. You install it from a file called something like
`bench-clock-1.3.0.apk`. It takes about two minutes. You need Android 8 or newer, which covers most
phones from the last 6–7 years.

Menu names vary a little between phone brands (Samsung, Pixel, Motorola and so on), but the steps
are the same.

## 1. Download the app file

Open the link or message you were sent **on your phone** and tap the `.apk` file to download it.
If your browser asks whether to keep the file, tap **Download** or **Download anyway**. Browsers say
this about every app file; it doesn't mean anything is wrong.

If you were sent a `.zip` file instead, open it in the **Files** app and tap the `.apk` inside.

## 2. Allow your phone to install it

Open the downloaded file: tap the download notification, or find it in the **Files** app under
**Downloads**.

The first time, Android will say something like *"For your security, your phone is currently not
allowed to install unknown apps from this source."*

1. Tap **Settings**.
2. Turn on **Allow from this source** (on Samsung: **Allow permission**).
3. Press **Back** to return to the installer.

This permission only applies to the app you opened the file from (for example Chrome, Files or
WhatsApp). You can switch it off again afterwards in **Settings → Apps → Special app access →
Install unknown apps**.

## 3. Install

Tap **Install**. When it finishes, tap **Open**, or find **Bench Clock** (green and gold stopwatch
icon) on your home screen or in your app list.

## What the Play Protect warning means

Google Play Protect checks apps that don't come from the Play Store. Because Bench Clock was built by
a parent and not published through Google, Play Protect doesn't recognise it and may show a warning
such as **"App scan recommended"**, **"Unsafe app blocked"** or **"Play Protect doesn't recognise
this app's developer"**. This happens to any app that isn't in the Play Store. It doesn't mean
Play Protect found anything harmful.

Bench Clock doesn't use the internet at all, doesn't ask for any permissions apart from vibration,
and keeps your teams only on your phone.

To continue:

- If you see **Scan app**, you can tap it. The scan comes back clean, then tap **Install**.
- If you see **More details** (or a small down-arrow), tap it, then tap **Install anyway**.
- If you're asked **Send app for scanning?**, either answer is fine.

Only install Bench Clock from the person who normally sends it to you. If someone you don't know
sends you an app file, don't install it.

## Updating to a new version

When you're sent a newer file (e.g. `bench-clock-1.4.0.apk`):

1. **Don't uninstall the old app.** Uninstalling deletes your teams.
2. Download and open the new file, the same way as above.
3. Tap **Update** (it may say **Install**). Your teams, logos and any game in progress are kept.

To be safe before updating, you can make a backup: in Bench Clock open **Team & game setup**, tap
**Export file**, and save the file to Google Drive or your Files.

If Android says **"App not installed"** or that the package **conflicts with an existing package**,
the file wasn't signed by the same person as your current app. Don't uninstall to get around it.
Ask whoever sent it for the correct file.

## Copying teams to another parent's phone

1. On your phone: **Team & game setup → Export file**, then send the file (WhatsApp, email, etc.).
2. On their phone: save the file, then in Bench Clock open **Team & game setup → Import file** and
   choose it. Then tap the red **Replace my teams…** button, and tap it again to confirm.

Importing **replaces** all the teams on the receiving phone. Backups from the iPhone version of
Bench Clock work too, and so does **Copy backup** / **Paste backup** if you'd rather paste than send a file.
